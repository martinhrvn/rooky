# Gamification — idea dump

XP, an achievement layer that feeds it, and a sticker album with a canvas she
can arrange. Nothing here is built yet. This file is the thinking, broken into
steps that can be picked up one at a time. Read `AGENTS.md` first — most of the
hard constraints below come from there, and every one of them still applies.

**Why bother:** Emma likes unlocking upgrades to her character in Chess for
Kids. Stars already reward *doing a level well*; there is nothing yet that
rewards *playing a lot*, *coming back*, or *doing a particular kind of thing*.
That is the gap.

## The loop, in one line

**Everything she does gives XP → the bar fills → she picks one of three
stickers → she sticks it on her canvas.**

One currency, one loop, one hop from cause to effect. That last part is the
reason this shape is right for a four-year-old: an earlier draft of this file
had two parallel reward systems (XP driving a character, achievements
separately awarding stickers), and a child who cannot read has to be *taught*
a two-currency economy. This one she can work out by playing it twice.

Achievements feed that loop rather than sitting beside it: they pay **a one-off
lump of XP**. Finishing a level gives a normal amount; crossing *Never give up*
gives a bigger one. Same bar, same stickers, just a spike — but the achievement
is still **announced in its own right**, because a bonus she cannot perceive is
not a reward. Step 3.

## The constraints this has to survive

Written out first, because every step below gets decided by one of them.

1. **She cannot read.** A reward is a *picture*. Its name is for the adult. If
   the reward does not say what it is on its own, it is not an achievement, it
   is a line of text with a frame around it.
2. **Counts are pips, not numerals.** So there is no `Level 7` and no
   `1240 XP` anywhere she can see. XP is a *bar filling*. Numerals stay on the
   path (ordinals) and in settings (adult-facing).
3. **Gold is rewards only, and stars own gold.** An XP bar in gold competes
   with the thing it is meant to be subordinate to. XP needs its own colour —
   see Step 4.
4. **Nothing she can reach may destroy progress**, and **nothing may punish
   her**. No streak that breaks, no daily-login pressure, no timer, no XP that
   goes down. She is four. A broken streak is a punishment for her parents
   being busy on a Tuesday.
5. **Rewards must never gate content.** Unlocking is lenient by design —
   "stars are for pride, not access" (`selectors.ts`). XP and stickers unlock
   *stickers*, never levels, tiers or worlds.
6. **Licence hygiene.** Any new artwork gets an `ASSETS.md` entry in the same
   commit, and nothing copyleft enters the tree. This is now a real decision
   rather than a footnote, because a bundled emoji set is thousands of files
   with a licence attached — see Step 7.
7. **Per profile, always.** Everything keyed off `activeProfileId`, like
   results. One child's album must be invisible to her sibling, and so must her
   canvas.
8. **Additive and migrated.** New persisted shape means `PROGRESS_VERSION` 4
   and a branch in `migrateProgress`. A migration that has never run is a
   migration you do not have.

## Step 0 — Decided

Settled on 2026-08-14. Recorded here so they are not re-opened by accident;
each one is expanded in its own step.

- **Achievements are a visible event, not a silent bonus.** Crossing one shows
  a notification — its icon and its name, e.g. *Never give up* — and then the
  XP bar fills. The XP is the reward; the notification is the recognition, and
  the two are not the same thing. Step 3.
- **The reward is a choice of three stickers.** When the bar completes, she is
  offered three she does not own and picks one. Agency beats randomness at
  four, choosing is itself a pleasure, and there is no "I wanted the dragon and
  got a pear" moment. Step 4.
- **The cost per sticker is constant.** No stretching curve — to a
  four-year-old that reads as the app quietly getting stingier. Step 2.
- **Rate: about one sticker every five levels.** Slower than the first guess of
  three, and better for it: each sticker matters more, the album lasts months
  rather than weeks, and five levels is still well within one sitting. Step 2.
- **OpenMoji** for the sticker art, accepting CC BY-SA 4.0. Step 7 spells out
  what that obligates.
- **Endless awards XP** at a lower rate and still writes no level result. It
  stays unscored in the sense that matters, while messing about with a knight
  still fills the bar.
- **Character levelling is parked.** The album is the reward; a second axis
  would dilute it. Step 6.

Still open:

- **What happens when the album is full?** Size it so it is not, then decide.
  Duplicates are meaningless because placement is already unlimited. If it
  completes, the bar fills and sits full — honest, and a nice problem to have.
  Do not invent prestige.

## Step 1 — Persisted shape and migration

**Files:** `src/progress/schema.ts`, `src/progress/store.ts`,
`src/progress/migrate.test.ts`, `src/progress/store.test.ts`

- Bump `PROGRESS_VERSION` to 4, add the `version < 4` branch to
  `migrateProgress`. Existing profiles get zero XP and an empty album — *not*
  back-filled from their results. (Back-filling is tempting and is Step 3's
  problem; see the note there.)
- New per-profile records, alongside `results`:

  ```ts
  /** profileId -> total XP ever earned. Monotonic; never decremented. */
  readonly xp: Readonly<Record<string, number>>;
  /** profileId -> counterId -> tally. Monotonic. Feeds the achievements. */
  readonly counters: Readonly<Record<string, Readonly<Record<string, number>>>>;
  /** profileId -> streakId -> current run. Resets on the breaking event. */
  readonly streaks: Readonly<Record<string, Readonly<Record<string, number>>>>;
  /** profileId -> achievementId -> earnedAt. One-shot; the XP is paid once. */
  readonly earned: Readonly<Record<string, Readonly<Record<string, number>>>>;
  /** profileId -> sticker ids owned, in the order they arrived. */
  readonly album: Readonly<Record<string, readonly string[]>>;
  /** profileId -> the three ids offered but not yet chosen from. See Step 4. */
  readonly pending: Readonly<Record<string, readonly string[]>>;
  /** profileId -> where she has stuck them. See Step 5. */
  readonly canvas: Readonly<Record<string, readonly Placement[]>>;
  ```

  Rationale for keeping them separate from `results` rather than nesting: the
  reset/delete paths in `store.ts` already key by profile id, so seven flat
  records means each of those paths is one more line, not a rewrite.

- **`streaks` is the one exception to "counters are monotonic"**, and it exists
  because *Never give up* — five failures in a row — is a run, not a total. Two
  notes on it. It resets on the **good** event (clearing the level), never on a
  bad one, so a reset is never a punishment; and because `earned` is one-shot,
  a reset can never take an achievement back off her. Keep the two records
  separate rather than overloading `counters`, so "monotonic" stays true of
  everything in `counters` and stays assertable in a test.
- **`pending` is not an optimisation, it is a safety net.** She will be offered
  three stickers and wander off without picking one — she is four. Persisting
  the offer means the choice is still waiting the next time she opens the
  album, instead of a reward evaporating because she got distracted. Nothing
  she can do may lose a sticker she has earned.

- **Store total XP, derive everything else.** How many stickers she has, how
  full the bar is, which sticker is next — all of it falls out of one integer
  plus the album order. Storing "current level" and "XP within level"
  separately is two numbers that can disagree, and the day they disagree is the
  day a child loses a sticker.
- `album` is stored explicitly rather than derived from XP even so, because
  Step 7 may reorder or extend the sticker list and **a sticker she already has
  must never turn into a different one**.
- `deleteProfile` must clear all seven. `resetProgress` — **decide**: does
  "start over" wipe XP, album and canvas? *Recommendation: yes for XP and the
  album — it is the parent-facing reset and a half-reset is confusing to
  explain.* **Hesitate over the canvas**: it is the only thing in the app she
  *made* rather than earned. If anything survives a reset it should be that.
  Whatever is decided, say exactly what it clears in
  `strings.settings.startOverHelp`.
- Store actions: `awardXp(amount)`, `bump(counterId)`, `extendStreak(id)` /
  `breakStreak(id)`, `offerStickers(ids)` / `chooseSticker(id)`,
  `place(placement)` / `unplace(key)`. `awardXp` returns what it caused —
  `{ before, after, fills: number }` — so the UI can play the right celebration
  without re-deriving it, and so **one award can complete the bar twice** (a
  big achievement bonus on top of a level) and offer two choices in turn rather
  than silently dropping one.

**Done when:** `migrate.test.ts` proves a v3 payload with real results comes
out the other side with its results intact and everything new empty.

## Step 2 — XP, as pure functions

**Files:** new `src/progress/xp.ts` + `xp.test.ts`

Pure, no React, no store — the same shape as `selectors.ts`, and testable in
vitest. This is the spine of the whole feature now, so it is worth getting the
numbers written down in one place where they can be argued about.

- `xpForResult(level, stars, moves, isFirstClear): number`
  - First clear of a level is worth most. A replay is worth a little, always
    more than zero, and **never zero for a level she loves** — she will replay
    the pawn level forty times and a bar that goes nowhere reads as broken.
  - Stars scale it. Over par does not reduce it below the base — "over par
    never fails a level", and it must not fail the bar either.
  - Suggested shape, to be tuned against real play: first clear 30, replay 8,
    +5 per star above one, +10 on a tier-3 level.
- `xpForEndlessWin(): number` — small and flat, per Step 0.
- `XP_PER_STICKER` — one constant, **flat, and it never grows**. With the
  numbers above a level averages roughly 40, so **200** puts a sticker at about
  every fifth level. That is the decided rate: slower than the first guess of
  three, and better for it — each sticker matters more and the album lasts
  months rather than weeks, while five levels is still comfortably one sitting.
- `stickersFor(totalXp): number` — `floor(xp / XP_PER_STICKER)`, and the bar is
  the remainder. Two lines, and they are the only place the arithmetic lives.

**Sanity-check the album length against this.** At five levels a sticker, the
authored campaign alone is worth a few dozen; achievements and Endless add
more. An album of sixty to eighty slots lasts without ever showing her an
empty second half. Work it out with the real level count before choosing how
many stickers to bundle in Step 7 — that number is also the bundle size.

**Watch for:** XP must be *derived-safe*. If a future version retunes the award
table, stored totals stay what they were — never recompute a total from
history, or a tuning change silently takes a sticker back off a child.

## Step 3 — Achievements: tallies that pay a bonus

**Files:** new `src/progress/achievements.ts` (catalogue + pure evaluation),
`achievements.test.ts`, plus the move and rewind hooks in
`src/ui/LevelPlayer.tsx`

An achievement is a running tally crossing a threshold — 5, 20, 100 — and what
it pays is **a one-shot lump of XP**. That is the whole integration: no
separate reward, no parallel economy, and the bar visibly jumping is what says
something special happened.

**The one-shot part is load-bearing.** It is what makes it safe to reward
failure with XP at all: "taken 25 times" pays once, so there is no loop where
hanging a queen repeatedly farms the bar. Per-event XP for a failure would be
exactly that loop. **Never pay XP per failure event; only at a threshold.**

### The doing tallies — a verb crossed with a piece

This is the generative one and it should carry most of the catalogue. Every
entry is *(piece × what it did)*, which makes the recognition icon trivial —
the piece, plus the glyph for the verb:

- **Moved with X** — one per piece. The knight's is *jumps*, and jumping 100
  times with a knight is the single most Emma-shaped achievement in this list.
- **Taken with X** — one per piece. "Taken 20 times with a pawn" is a real
  chess habit and not an easy one, because a pawn takes diagonally.
- **Slid the length of the board** with a rook, bishop or queen — the long
  move, which is what those pieces are *for*.
- **Promoted a pawn** — 1 / 5 / 25. It already exists as a phase in the engine.
- **Gave check** and **delivered mate** — from the check and mate worlds.
- **Collected stars**, **cleared levels**, **replayed a level**.

Two things fall out of this shape for free. It rewards **the pieces she
likes**, not only forward progress — a child who adores the knight and keeps
going back to it should be collecting something for it, and today she collects
nothing. And it gives **Endless a purpose**: it is a knight-jump farm, and that
is now a feature rather than a mode that earns nothing.

**Watch the thresholds against reality.** A level is about three moves, so 100
knight jumps is roughly thirty-five knight levels including replays and
Endless — reachable, but not in a week. This is the reason the counters ship
early (see the build order): guessing these numbers before there is a fortnight
of her real play is the part of this plan most likely to be wrong.

### The failure tallies, and why they exist

Separate section because the reason matters and will otherwise be lost:
**Emma is sometimes afraid of failing.** Rewarding the failures is a way to
take the sting out — if losing a piece is a thing that *pays*, then losing a
piece is a thing that is *supposed to happen*, and the app says so rather than
the parent having to. Twenty-five losses is not a failure state, it is
twenty-five attempts.

- **Taken** — her piece got captured (`phase: 'lost'`). 5 / 10 / 25. Cumulative.
- **Stranded** — the pawn-has-no-move rewind. Rarer: 1 / 5 / 10. Its own tally
  rather than folded into Taken, because it is a different mistake.
- ***Never give up*** — **five failures in a row on the same level**, then
  clearing it. This is the one that matters most and it is a **streak**, not a
  total: it says *you stayed with the hard one*, which is a different and
  better thing than *you have lost a lot over time*.
  - It is a `streaks` entry (Step 1), reset by clearing the level rather than
    by anything bad, so the reset is never felt as a loss.
  - **Decide whether the badge fires on the fifth failure or on the eventual
    win.** *Recommendation: on the win.* Firing mid-struggle interrupts the
    exact moment she is most frustrated, with a notification she cannot read;
    firing on the win turns the whole grind into the story of that win. It also
    keeps the rule from Step 4 that nothing pops up mid-level.
  - Tiers at 5 / 10 attempts, so the really stubborn afternoon pays twice.

The app is already half-way to this: it refuses to flash "wrong", plays the
capture out, and rewinds one move. Paying out on the twenty-fifth is that same
decision carried to its conclusion.

**Tone: warm, never a joke at her expense.** It says *you kept going*, not
*you lost again*. 💪 🌱 🛟, not 💀. The names are adult-facing
(`strings`/screen reader) but a parent reads them aloud — do not ship
"Blunderer".

### And a few odds and ends

- **Hint used** — 1 / 5 / 25. Reward it, and for the same reason as the failure
  ones. The hint shows the danger, which is the thing she is there to learn to
  see; an app that quietly shames her for pressing it teaches the opposite of
  what the hint is for.
- **Mix rounds** and **Endless rounds** — separate tallies, so each mode has
  something only it can give. Reinforces that the two are different in kind.
- One per **piece world** and per **theme world** completed. These fall
  straight out of `results` rather than needing a counter, so they are nearly
  free.

### The notification — the achievement is its own event

**Decided:** an achievement that only paid XP would be invisible to her. She
cannot read, so "crossed 100 knight jumps" and "finished a level" would both
look like the bar moving, one just further. So the achievement gets a moment of
its own: **its icon and its name appear, and then the XP bar fills.** The XP is
the reward; the notification is the recognition, and they are not the same
thing.

- The **name is shown even though she cannot read it** — this is one of the few
  places that is right, because an adult is usually nearby when something
  unusual happens on screen, and *Never give up* is a thing a parent will read
  out loud. That reading-aloud is most of the value. The icon still has to
  carry it alone when nobody is watching.
- Names in `strings.ts` like everything else, and warm rather than clinical:
  *Never give up*, not *Failure streak 5*.
- Then the bar fills, visibly further than a normal level does. The size of the
  jump is the only "how big was that" signal she gets, so make the bonus large
  enough to see — a bonus that fills a tenth of the bar reads as noise.

### Wiring the counters

The piece tallies mean counting **every move**, not just the notable ones, so
resist a bump call per achievement — twenty scattered `bump()` calls in the
board screen is how these drift out of sync.

- **One event, one pure mapping.** `LevelPlayer` emits a single move event —
  roughly `{ piece, captured?, promoted?, gaveCheck?, from, to }`, all of which
  the engine already knows when it applies the move — and a pure
  `countersFor(event): readonly string[]` in `achievements.ts` turns it into
  the counter ids to bump. All the *(piece × verb)* logic lives in that one
  function, testable without React or a store.
- Three emit sites in `LevelPlayer.tsx`, one per kind of thing that happens:
  the completed move (in the `tap`/`promote` handling, ~line 220, where it
  already compares `next.moves > s.moves`), the capture-rewind effect
  (`state.phase === 'lost'`, ~line 162) and the stranded-rewind effect
  (~line 194). Each already fires exactly once per event; do not add a fourth
  counting path.
- **A rewound move still counts as a move.** She jumped the knight; the board
  taking it back does not unjump it. This also keeps the counters monotonic,
  which is the property the whole thing rests on.
- `LevelPlayer` currently takes `onWin` and knows nothing about the store. Keep
  it that way — pass `onMove` / `onLost` / `onStranded` in the same style and
  let the three screens decide.

### Evaluation

- One pure function: `newlyEarned(counters, results, alreadyEarned) -> ids`.
  Pure, no store, no React. The caller pays the XP for each and records it in
  `earned`, which is what makes it one-shot.
- **Counters cannot be back-filled and that is fine.** There is no record of
  how many times she has been taken before this ships, so every tally starts at
  zero for everyone, including a child who has finished four worlds. Say so out
  loud rather than trying to estimate one from `results`.
- The `results`-derived ones (piece worlds, theme worlds) **can** be
  back-filled and should be — silently, and **without paying XP**, on the first
  evaluation after hydration. Paying them would dump six stickers on a
  returning player in one launch, which is not a celebration, it is a mess.

## Step 4 — The bar, and the moment it fills

**Files:** `src/ui/TierRank.tsx` (repurpose), new `src/ui/XpBar.tsx`, new
`src/ui/StickerAward.tsx`, `app/index.tsx`, `src/ui/LevelPlayer.tsx`,
`src/ui/strings.ts`, `src/ui/theme.ts`

- **Where the bar lives.** Home header, next to the avatar. Also on the win
  screen, because that is where it moves and a bar that fills off-screen is a
  reward she never sees.
- **What it is drawn as.** Tried as `TierRank` — eight board squares filling
  in — and **rejected on sight**: the alternating fill read as a checkerboard
  rather than as progress, and fought the one thing the bar has to say, which
  is *how close*. It is now a plain continuous jade bar, and it is better.
  - A count was the wrong idea here anyway. The move dots are countable
    because "how many moves" is the question; nobody tallies an XP bar.
  - **This leaves `AGENTS.md`'s open sore open.** The signature element still
    lives only on `app/pieces.tsx`, which nothing links to. The XP bar was
    going to be its home and is not. Either find it another one or accept a
    different signature and say so — do not leave it drifting.
- **Colour.** Not gold — gold is stars. Jade, or a dedicated accent in
  `theme.ts`; if a new accent goes in, check `inkOnAccent` contrast before
  shipping it, per `AGENTS.md`.
- **The award moment**, in order: stars settle → *(achievement notification, if
  one landed)* → bar fills → *(if it completed, the choice of three)*.
  - **Skippable with the same tap that skips the celebration** — up to the
    choice. Levels get replayed constantly; a second unskippable cutscene on
    top of the first is worse than the first was.
  - **Two fills at once is possible** — a big achievement bonus landing on top
    of a level's XP can complete the bar twice. Handle it as two choices in
    turn, never as a pile of popups.

### The choice of three

**Decided:** when the bar completes she is offered three stickers she does not
own, and picks one. This is the moment the whole feature builds to, so:

- **Three, face up, all equally good.** No rarity, no "best" pick — the point
  is agency, and a wrong answer would poison it. She cannot get this decision
  wrong, which is exactly why it is a decision she is allowed to make.
- **Big targets, drawn large.** This is the one screen where the stickers are
  shown at their best size; the album shows them small.
- **The offer is persisted before it is shown** (`pending`, Step 1). She will
  wander off mid-choice. When she comes back — next launch, or opening the
  album — the same three are still waiting. **A reward must never evaporate
  because a four-year-old got distracted**, and this is the only place in the
  app where that could happen.
- **The three are drawn from the current album page**, so a page fills in a
  reasonable time rather than staying one slot short forever. Falls back to the
  whole unowned pool when a page is nearly done. Fewer than three left? Offer
  what remains.
- **Deterministic offer.** Derive the three from the fill index rather than
  `Math.random()`, so the same offer survives a reload and the tests are not
  flaky. It also stops a child re-rolling by force-quitting, which she will
  absolutely discover.
- **Never mid-level.** A failure bonus lands in the middle of the rewind
  animation, on top of the moment her piece got taken. Queue it for the end of
  the level, which is the right emotional order anyway.
- Every new string into `strings.ts`, every text node through `Text.tsx`.

## Step 5 — The album and the canvas

**The canvas half is built** — see the list at the top of this file and the
canvas section of `AGENTS.md`, which supersedes the sketch below wherever the
two differ (placements carry a background alongside them; the album became the
tray rather than a page of silhouettes; drag came in alongside tap). The
**album half is still open**: fixed slots with silhouettes ahead, and pages
with a loose theme, are unbuilt.

**Files:** new `app/stickers.tsx`, new `src/ui/StickerAlbum.tsx`, new
`src/ui/StickerCanvas.tsx`, `app/index.tsx`

Two halves on one screen: the **album** is what she owns (and, as silhouettes,
what is coming); the **canvas** is what she has made.

Why this is the best reward in the file, written down so it does not get
value-engineered away later:

- **A sticker needs no explanation.** A "badge" is an abstraction a
  four-year-old has to be taught; a sticker is a thing she has been peeling off
  sheets and sticking on furniture for two years. Constraint 1 is satisfied by
  the format itself rather than by careful icon design.
- **It is a toy, not a trophy cabinet.** A shelf is something you look at once.
  A canvas is something she *does*, and arranging it is play in its own right —
  a reason to come back to the rewards screen rather than glance at it.

### The canvas

- **Tap a sticker in the album to drop it on the canvas, then drag it.**
  `react-native-gesture-handler` and `reanimated` are both already
  dependencies, so nothing new enters the tree.
- **Unlimited copies.** Owning a sticker means placing it any number of times —
  she can cover the canvas in twenty-five knights. One-sticker-one-placement
  creates "I lost my sticker" grief for no gain, and grief is exactly what this
  feature is not for.
- **Placements are normalised, not pixels.**
  `Placement = { key, stickerId, x: 0..1, y: 0..1, scale, rotation }`. Absolute
  pixels break the moment the device rotates or it is opened on a tablet, and a
  canvas that rearranges itself is a canvas that destroyed something she made.
- **Removing one is a drag off the canvas**, one at a time, and it removes the
  *placement* only — the sticker stays owned forever. **No "clear the canvas"
  button anywhere she can reach**: it is the one control here that could wipe
  an afternoon's work in a mis-tap, and there is no confirm dialog she could
  read. If a clear-all is wanted at all it goes behind the gear.
- **Gestures, in order of how sure they are to work at four.** Tap to place and
  drag to move are safe and are v1. Pinch to resize and two-finger rotate are
  not — stretch goals, and never the only way to do anything. Rotation is the
  one worth having, because a wonky sticker is what makes the canvas look like
  *hers*.
- **What it looks like.** Her screen rather than the play screen, so it can be
  warmer than the chrome — but it still may not out-shout the board.
  *Recommendation: a `surface` panel with a faint board grid*, so the stickers
  read as stuck onto something rather than floating in the dark.

### The album

- **Fixed slots with silhouettes ahead.** Every sticker has a permanent place
  in the album, and the ones she does not own yet show as silhouettes. She can
  see what is missing, the filled part is a picture of how far she has come,
  and — since the choice offers three of those unowned ones — the silhouettes
  are what makes the choice feel like *picking*, rather than being handed
  something from nowhere.
- **Pages of six or eight** with a loose theme — animals, space, food. A page
  completing is a free extra beat of satisfaction, it keeps a long album from
  reading as one endless strip, and it is what the choice draws from.
- **Reached by tapping her own avatar on home.** It is hers, so it lives next
  to her face, not in the parent's settings. That tap currently opens the
  profile switcher, so **decide**: the avatar opens the album and switching
  moves to a small control inside it, or the album gets its own home button.
  *Recommendation: the former* — switching profile is occasional, this is daily.
- **Never empty.** The first level cleared should land the first sticker, so
  the first time she opens it there is already something to stick. An empty
  canvas is the worst possible first impression of the whole feature.
- **Stickers are stored by id, never by character or filename**, so the set can
  be re-arted later without orphaning a saved canvas, and an unknown id falls
  back rather than rendering a blank. Same rule as `avatarById`, same reason.

## Step 7 — The sticker artwork: OpenMoji

**Decided: OpenMoji**, accepting **CC BY-SA 4.0**. It is the best-looking
option for this app — the outlined style sits against the dark chrome the way
nothing flat-filled does, and it is drawn to be read small, which is what an
album of sixty needs.

It was chosen with the licence understood rather than in spite of it. Twemoji
(CC BY 4.0) and Noto Emoji (Apache-2.0 / OFL) are both more permissive, and if
the share-alike ever becomes awkward, either is a drop-in replacement — which
is precisely why stickers are stored **by id, never by filename** (Step 5).

### What CC BY-SA 4.0 actually obligates

**Verify at the source in the commit that adds the assets** — this is from
memory, and emoji projects have re-licensed before.

- **Attribution**, in OpenMoji's requested form, in `ASSETS.md` *and* in the
  Credits section of settings. That section already discharges the Cburnett
  CC BY-SA obligation and exists for exactly this; `AGENTS.md` says not to
  remove it, and this is the second reason why.
- **Share-alike applies to the artwork, not to the app.** Shipping the SVGs
  unmodified inside an MIT app is fine — the app's code stays MIT and the App
  Store is unaffected. This is a second BY-SA obligation on assets, not a
  licence change.
- **Do not recolour or edit the files.** A modified OpenMoji is a derivative
  and must itself be released under BY-SA, which is a commitment worth avoiding
  for the sake of a hue tweak. If one *must* be edited, keep it in its own
  directory and say so in `ASSETS.md`, so the boundary between our artwork and
  theirs never becomes a research project.
- Prefer the **colour** set for stickers. OpenMoji's black outline set is
  gorgeous but would compete with the pieces, which are the only line art the
  app is supposed to have.

### Mechanics

- `react-native-svg-transformer` is already in devDependencies, so SVG stickers
  import as components — the path is paved.
- **Ship only the sixty or so actually used**, not the full set of several
  thousand. Bundle size is the whole reason to care, and Step 2 says to work
  the album length out from the real level count first.
- Any new SVG needs an explicit `viewBox`, or react-native-svg draws it at
  native coordinates and clips it. `AGENTS.md` flags this as the first thing to
  check when new artwork looks wrong; the Cburnett files shipped without one,
  so do not assume OpenMoji's have one either — **check before committing
  sixty files**.
- `ASSETS.md` entry **in the same commit**, per the standing rule.

## Step 8 — Adult-facing surfaces

**Files:** `app/settings.tsx`, `app/dev.tsx`

- **Settings:** a switch to turn the whole thing off? *Probably yes.* Some
  parents will not want an XP bar in a teaching app, and the flag is far
  cheaper now than retrofitted. If it goes in: off hides the bar, the award
  moment and the album, and XP keeps accruing underneath so turning it back on
  is not a punishment.
- **Dev panel:** grant XP, set any counter or streak, force a sticker choice,
  grant the whole album. Forcing the choice is how that screen gets built
  without playing five levels between each attempt. Setting a
  counter directly is the only sane way to test a threshold at 100 — the
  alternative is jumping a knight a hundred times by hand — and a full album is
  how the canvas gets designed against sixty stickers rather than three. It
  already writes straight to saved progress with no undo, behind seven taps on
  the version line; that is the right place and the only place.
- **A "clear the canvas" control, if there is one at all, lives here.**

## Step 9 — Tests and gates

- `xp.test.ts` — awards are never negative, a replay is never zero,
  `stickersFor` is monotonic in XP, the cost per sticker is the *same* at the
  first and the fiftieth (the constant-cost decision, asserted rather than
  trusted), and one award that completes the bar twice reports two fills.
- `achievements.test.ts` — every catalogue entry is reachable (a state exists
  that earns it), ids are unique, thresholds within one tally strictly ascend,
  and a maxed-out state earns all of them. The unreachable check is the same
  idea as the solver content gate: it catches the achievement nobody can ever
  get.
- **The one that protects the design:** an achievement pays **at most once**.
  Evaluate twice against the same state and assert the second pass awards zero
  XP. That single test is what stops "reward the failures" turning into "farm
  the bar by losing".
- `countersFor(event)` — every counter id in the catalogue is produced by some
  event, and every id it produces is in the catalogue. That pair of checks
  stops a *(piece × verb)* achievement nothing ever increments, which is the
  failure mode this shape invites.
- **Streaks** — resetting one never removes an `earned` entry. *Never give up*
  survives clearing the level, which is the event that resets the streak that
  granted it, and that ordering is easy to get wrong.
- **The choice** — the three offered are always unowned, always distinct,
  deterministic for a given fill index (so no flaky test and no re-roll by
  force-quit), and degrade gracefully to two or one when the pool runs low.
- **`pending` survives a reload.** Offer three, drop the store, rehydrate, and
  assert the same three are still on offer. This is the test that protects the
  "a reward never evaporates because she got distracted" rule.
- `migrate.test.ts` — v3 payload survives, results untouched, everything new
  empty.
- `store.test.ts` — `partialize` still carries `activeProfileId` *and* the new
  keys; delete-profile clears all seven; `bump` and `awardXp` are monotonic and
  per-profile (one profile's award leaves the sibling's alone); a placement
  round-trips through persistence unchanged, which is the test that catches
  normalised coordinates being stored as pixels.
- `npm test`, `npm run typecheck`, and `npx expo export --platform android`
  before calling any step done — the last one because bundling sixty SVGs is
  exactly the kind of change that breaks the native build and nothing else.

## Deliberately not doing

Written down so it does not get re-litigated in six months.

- **No streaks, no daily goals, no "come back tomorrow".** Constraint 4.
- **No leaderboards, no sharing, no anything that leaves the device.** There is
  no server and no data collection, and that is a feature.
- **No lives, no energy, no second currency to spend.** One bar, one album.
  Every one of those mechanics exists to make someone play longer than they
  want to. She is four.
- **No numerals on the bar**, however tempting. If it cannot say it without a
  number, redesign it.
- **No content behind a sticker.** Cosmetics only.
- **No stickers she can lose, trade or spend.** Earned, kept, placed as often
  as she likes. The moment a sticker is scarce, arranging the canvas stops
  being play and becomes a decision she can get wrong.
- **No XP for a failure *event*** — only for crossing a failure *threshold*,
  once. Step 3 explains why this one is not negotiable.

## Suggested order to actually build it

1. **Step 1** (shape + migration) and **Step 2** (XP arithmetic). Nothing
   visible, fully tested.
2. **Step 3's counters** — the pure `countersFor` mapping and the three emit
   sites. Still nothing visible, but the tallies start accruing on the real
   device from that day, and every day this lands earlier is a day of real
   numbers to aim the thresholds at. It is also the cheapest step in the file.
3. **Steps 4 and 5 with system emoji as placeholders** — bar, award moment,
   choice of three, album, canvas. This is the whole loop end to end and it
   should be in her hands before anything is polished. Placeholders because the
   loop is what needs testing, and because sixty OpenMoji files are easier to
   commit once the album length is known.
4. **The rest of Step 3** — the achievement catalogue and the notification,
   with a fortnight of real tallies to set thresholds against. *Never give up*
   is the one to build first and watch her hit.
5. **Step 7** — swap the placeholders for OpenMoji, with the `ASSETS.md` entry
   and the credits line in the same commit.
6. **Step 8**, then settle Step 0's one remaining open question (what a full
   album does) with something playable in front of you.

If only one thing from this file ever ships, it should be steps 1–3.
